## The TP/SL Balance Problem

If you have never tried to tune a trading bot, let me explain the pain. You have two numbers that control everything: your take profit (TP) and your stop loss (SL). Set TP too tight and you close winners early while your losers run. Set SL too tight and you get stopped out by noise before the trade plays out. Set both too wide and you are just holding positions with no plan.

I have been running my bot on [Strike Finance](https://app.strikefinance.org/referral/accept?code=75cc56269fa647f58e1d5de53ec69639) for a while now, and I just went through a painful lesson in asymmetric risk reward.

Here is what happened. My win rate was sitting at 85%. Sounds amazing right? Except I was making $2 on winners and losing $6 on losers. The math does not care about your win rate if the losses are 3x the wins. I was running a TP of 2.5% ROE with stop losses ranging from 7 to 13% ROE depending on leverage. Every win was a small step forward and every loss was a big step back.

## The Fix Nobody Talks About

Everyone in crypto trading talks about entries. The signal, the setup, the confirmation. But the real edge is in exit management. I had to go back to basics:

**Step 1:** Map actual trade outcomes. Not what the backtest said, what really happened in production. My ZEC longs were getting destroyed. The margin boost I added to capitalize on bounces was amplifying losses instead.

**Step 2:** Fix the asymmetry. I bumped TP from 2.5% to 5.0% ROE. This means I need a lower win rate to be profitable, but each winner actually moves the needle. A 60% win rate at 5% TP and 7% SL is better than an 85% win rate at 2.5% TP and 10% SL.

**Step 3:** Remove the fancy stuff that was hurting more than helping. I had a bounce detector and a 2x margin boost for specific tokens. They looked good in theory but in practice they were concentrating risk in the worst spots.

This is the grind. You build something, watch it run, see it fail in a way you did not expect, fix it, and repeat. There is no final version. Just the next version.
