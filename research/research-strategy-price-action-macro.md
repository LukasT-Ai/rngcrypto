# Strategy research: price-action direction and macro integration for the signal engine

Date: 2026-10-13. Scope: `src/lib/signals/engine.ts` (scoreFromCandles, computeMultiFactorCall, computeTaSystem, computeRegime, stop/TP ladder), `src/lib/ta/*`, `src/lib/macro/*`, `scripts/backtest.ts`. Assets: BTC, ETH, alts, GOLD, WTI. Timeframes 15m/1h/4h/1d, holding hours to days.

This report does not re-recommend what already exists (regime factor, HTF short gate, squeeze WAIT gate, structural confirmation for reversals, event-weight decay, counter-regime gate, symmetric macro bonus). It targets the gaps the code and the backtest expose, and every section ends with rules the existing candles-only harness can test with a flag.

---

## 0. What the engine does today and where the structural leaks are

Read of the current scoring path, with the consequences that drive the recommendations below.

**Scoring is a single weighted sum of ~18 category scores, normalized by the weights in play, then confidence = 50 + |score|/2.** (engine.ts ~3350-3525.) The grade ladder and the publish gate run on that confidence. Nothing in the chain is a calibrated probability.

**The categories mix opposite philosophies in one sum.** In the candles-only path `macroBias` is null, so `scoreMomentum` is pure mean reversion (RSI < 30 scores +35, RSI > 70 scores -35, Stoch K < 20 +20) and `scoreBollinger` is pure mean reversion (lower band +35). Meanwhile `scoreMarketStructure` (EMA stack ±40, Supertrend ±25, price vs EMA9 ±10), `scoreHTF` (three EMA-trend votes plus alignment), `computeRegime` (cloud/200MA/ADX) and the trend half of `computeTaSystem` are all trend continuation. In a trend, momentum and Bollinger vote against structure, HTF and regime, and the sum compresses toward zero. The published WAIT rate and the Jun-Aug 2026 negative window are partly this: the engine is averaging a trend system and a fade system instead of choosing one.

**Trend is counted at least five times.** EMA9/21/50 stack, Supertrend, price > EMA9, 1h/4h/1d EMA trend in `scoreHTF`, cloud + 200MA + ADX in `computeRegime`, and Ichimoku/Chandelier/PSAR inside the taSystem trend composite. Combined weight of trend-flavoured inputs is roughly 0.13 + 0.08 + 0.05 + ~0.05 (taSystem trend share) = 0.31 before Elliott. The effective breadth of the composite is far lower than 18 inputs suggests, which is exactly the Grinold-Kahn warning that correlated signals do not add breadth ([quantmemo](https://quantmemo.com/writing/paper-grinold-fundamental-law), [Signal correlation, IC and PnL dependence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09588)).

**Levels are pure swing pivots** (`findSwingLevelsFromCandles`, lookback 2, deduped at 0.5%). Entry snaps to the nearest support within 2 ATR, stop hides behind it with a 0.5 ATR buffer and an 0.8 ATR floor, TP1 is the nearest resistance, TP2/TP3 the next ones or +1 ATR steps. There is no VWAP/AVWAP, no session level, no prior-day high/low in the ladder (the daily/weekly highs are computed in `computeTimeframeLevels` but only reported).

**The exit model is fixed**: TP ladder, hard stop, 24h horizon (HORIZON_MS in backtest.ts), stop-after-TP exits at the last TP. No breakeven move, no trail, no timeframe-dependent horizon. A 4h/1d setup and a 15m setup get the same 24h clock.

**Macro factors are three additive scores** (macro 0.08, event 0.12 boosted x2 for 30 min then decaying to base over 4h with a 2h half-life, confirmation 0.08) plus a confidence bonus from the daily regime, a 30-minute pre/post blackout (EVENT_BLACKOUT_MS), and a 0.85 confidence cap inside 15 minutes of a high-importance print. The event score keeps a directional vote for up to 6h after a release.

**The backtest reports bySymbol and alignment but has no regime, session or month buckets, no breakeven or trailing option, and the 36-config sweep was not deflated for selection.**

## 1. Price-action strategy families: evidence, parameters, failure regimes, and when to use which

### 1.1 Trend following with regime filters (200MA / ADX / Cloud)

**Evidence quality: moderate to good, the best of any family here.** Time-series momentum in BTC is documented at daily and weekly horizons by Liu and Tsyvinski (RFS 2021) and Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu (one-week cross-sectional momentum about 2.7% weekly) ([NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf), [NBER w25882](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf)); later work finds the effect regime-dependent and weaker after 2018 ([Arefev, SSRN 7405060](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7405060), [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). The one directly relevant intraday test: Quantpedia's hourly BTC MACD with a daily-MACD direction filter and shorts disabled cut trades from 2,262 to about 1,000, raised annual return 4.6% to 6.6%, cut max drawdown 23.9% to 12.4% and raised Sharpe 0.33 to 0.80 ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-to-design-a-simple-multi-timeframe-trend-strategy-on-bitcoin/)). A 2,700-backtest ranking of 20 regime filters across SPY/QQQ/BTC found plain price-above-long-MA and price-within-x%-of-high filters cheap and robust across all three markets while fancier filters (TEMA, Hull, KAMA, "higher lows") cost 2-4 points a year for protection available more cheaply; "higher lows" made BTC worse. The filters were scored on the middle of their parameter range, not the best setting ([Setup4Alpha](https://setup4alpha.substack.com/p/i-tested-20-trend-based-regime-filters)). The classic Davey 567,000-backtest exit study found breakout entries worked best on daily and 720-minute bars and worst on 60-minute bars ([KJ Trading](https://kjtradingsystems.com/algo-trading-exits.html)).

**Parameters that generalize:** long MA lengths 100-300 bars (the ranking study excluded anything under 100 days and found results flat across the plateau); ADX > 20-25 as the "trend exists" line is convention not evidence, but the engine's own 60-day data (ADX < 20 squeeze gate) is consistent with it; the key is a wide plateau, not a point value.

**Failure regimes:** ranges and transitions (whipsaw across the MA), post-blow-off reversals (trend filter still on while structure breaks), low-ADX "above the cloud but going nowhere" which `computeRegime` already damps.

**When to use:** regime label Bull/Bear with blended ADX > 20. Take only continuation setups in the regime direction. This is where the engine's edge actually lives (the +0.18R Aug-Oct window, long-biased in uptrends).

### 1.2 Breakout and range strategies with volatility filters (squeeze, ATR percentiles, Donchian)

**Evidence quality: weak to moderate.** Published crypto squeeze/Donchian backtests are vendor-grade: ETH BB-breakout 21 trades, 33% win rate ([CoinQuant](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/breakout-trading-strategy-does-it-work-on-crypto-backtested)); BTC 20-period Donchian on 30m returned -55% ([CoinQuant 30m](https://www.coinquant.ai/strategies/btc-donchian-channel-30m-backtest)); one guide lists BTC 4h squeeze PF 1.61 over 85 trades with no method ([stratbase](https://stratbase.ai/en/blog/bollinger-bands-strategy-guide)). The robust finding across sources is structural: breakout win rates run 30-40% with 3-5x payoff, they die in ranges, and bar size matters (daily/720m good, 60m bad per Davey). Opening-range breakout in equities reproduces gross (+0.13R, 24% win on NQ) but is a coin flip after spread and slippage and has no out-of-sample period ([Zarattini & Aziz SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622), [MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235), [GitHub replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)).

**Parameters that generalize:** compression defined as BB width or ATR(14) at or below the 20th-30th percentile of the last 100 bars; breakout as a close beyond a 20-bar Donchian band; a direction filter from the higher timeframe. The ATR-percentile "trade only between the 20th and 90th percentile" rule is widespread but unvalidated ([TradingView script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/6he9Wwoe-Volatility-Regime-Filter-ATR-based/)).

**Failure regimes:** false breaks in low-ADX chop (the engine's squeeze gate already blocks these), and breakouts against the HTF regime.

**When to use:** only as a continuation entry trigger inside an established regime, on 1h/4h, never on 15m standalone. The engine currently treats "volatility_compression" only as a WAIT reason; the release of a squeeze in the regime direction is the untested half.

### 1.3 Mean reversion at HTF levels

**Evidence quality: moderate, and it is overwhelmingly regime-conditional.** A 78-backtest grid (BTC/ETH, 15m-1d, Bollinger k=2/2.5, RSI-2, distance-from-MA, long only, no filter) averaged +16.3% in bull, -2.2% sideways, -40.6% in bear regimes; 15m averaged -14.4% mainly from fees; 14 of 78 cells won 65%+ of trades and still lost money ([CoinQuant 78 backtests](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/building-a-mean-reversion-strategy-in-cryptocurrency-markets-evidence-from-78-backtests)). A daily stochastic reversion on BTC 2018-2026 made 27 trades, 74% win, PF 2.77, with the losers concentrated in strong trends; the author's own prescription is a 200-day MA switch between the two families ([CoinQuant MR vs TF](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/mean-reversion-crypto-strategy-backtested-when-it-beats-trend-following)). RSI mean reversion on BTC daily/4h over 2024-2026 produced Sharpe 0.23 and a 43.5% drawdown on 4h ([CoinQuant RSI](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/trading-the-fear-what-backtesting-an-rsi-mean-reversion-strategy-through-extreme-fear-reveals-btc-2026)). On SPY daily, a simple RSI-oversold cross had the strongest single t-stat (+3.42) of any pattern tested, on only 20 events ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)).

**Parameters that generalize:** reversion only toward the regime (buy dips above the long MA, sell rips below it); distance-from-MA in ATR units beat Bollinger and RSI-2 on risk-adjusted terms in the grid; 4h and 1d only.

**Failure regimes:** bear trends for longs (keeps buying a falling market), fee-driven bleed on 15m.

**When to use:** regime Bull or Mixed with ADX < 25 for longs at HTF support; mirror for shorts. This is precisely the condition under which the engine's momentum and Bollinger factors should be active, and the condition under which they should be muted is a strong-ADX trend.

### 1.4 Liquidity sweep and stop-hunt reversals (SMC/ICT)

**Evidence quality: poor as a standalone entry.** The only independent quantitative test (daily bars, SPY/QQQ/DIA/IWM, 648 backtests) found liquidity sweeps (N-bar low broken intrabar, close back above) had a +0.12% 5-day edge with t = 0.94, FVG fills t = -0.08, OTE t = -0.17; one of 32 ICT t-stats reached 2.0; zero of 648 backtests beat buy-and-hold and sweeps beat random entries in only 33% of variants ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)). The concept predates ICT (Wyckoff spring/upthrust, Raschke "Turtle Soup") and no academic work supports the "interbank algorithm" narrative ([tradingwyckoff](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/smart-money-concepts/)). Intraday application is untested rather than disproven.

**Implication for the engine:** keep sweeps and CHoCH where they are, as *structural confirmation* that a reversal read may carry full weight (`STRUCTURAL` set in `computeTaSystem`), not as a directional vote with its own weight. Do not raise their weight. Test their removal from the composite to see whether they are paying for themselves.

### 1.5 VWAP / anchored VWAP reversion

**Evidence quality: weak but directionally consistent.** Session-VWAP band fades show 57-63% win rates in equity tests (a QuantConnect 2-SD band short on 100 NASDAQ names, a JPM 2021 study of 4,200 trades) that could not be independently verified ([tradealgo](https://www.tradealgo.com/trading-guides/technical-analysis/vwap-trading-strategy-the-institutional-benchmark-every-trader-should-know)); the N-day VWAP cross test concludes reversion works short-run, trend long-run ([Quantified Strategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/vwap-trading-strategy/)). No anchored-VWAP test exists. The mechanical case is stronger than the empirical one: VWAP from a swing low/high or from a macro print is the one level institutional execution actually references, and it is computable from candles.

**When to use:** as a *location* feature (distance to AVWAP in ATR) that improves the stop/TP ladder and the mean-reversion entry, not as a vote. `vwapBands` already exists in `indicators.ts` and is unused by the composite.

### 1.6 Session-based setups (US open, Asia, London)

**Evidence quality: moderate for volatility and liquidity clustering, weak for direction.** Realised volatility and liquidity peak at the US and London cash opens and are thinnest in the early UTC morning ([Reading manuscript](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/81745/3/2.R&R.Manuscript.pdf), [arXiv 2109.12142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12142)). Returns on NYSE-open days accumulate overnight, not intraday ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)). A high-frequency trend ensemble on BTC (2018-2025, gross Sharpe ~1.6) earned its best performance from Sunday 19:00 NY through Monday (Tokyo open) and its worst on Sunday morning NY, with the effect much stronger after mid-2020 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)). Some peer-reviewed work finds no stable intraday return seasonality at all ([MDPI 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/13/16/2691)).

**Implication:** session is a *volatility and strategy-selector* feature, not a direction feature. Trend entries around the US/London opens and the Monday Asia open; fade entries and tighter risk in the Sunday chop and the 03:00-07:00 UTC lull. Cheapest first step is to bucket backtest results by entry hour and weekday to see whether the engine's own edge is session-concentrated.

### 1.7 Funding-driven squeezes

**Evidence quality: weak for direction, moderate for volatility.** Funding is itself forecastable ([Inan, SSRN 5576424](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5576424)) but its price predictability is small: R² 0.003 for 8h returns on BitMEX ([Fulgur](https://medium.com/@fulgur.ventures/bitcoin-funding-rates-and-price-predictability-27ce95535af1)), 12.5% of 7-day variance initially then fading, better cross-sectionally than for one asset ([Presto Labs](https://www.prestolabs.io/research/can-funding-rate-predict-price-change)); Coinbase's primer reads prolonged high funding as a *volatility* precursor, not a direction call ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional/research-insights/research/market-intelligence/a-primer-on-perpetual-futures)). Industry heuristics (funding > 15% APR for days, liquidations > 5% of OI) precede cascades but are untested ([Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/leverage-liquidations-the-31b-deleveraging)).

**Implication:** keep `scoreDerivatives` small; use funding extremes as a *stop-width and size* input (expect a cascade, widen the stop, cut size) and as a tie-breaker for which side of a squeeze release to take. Not testable candles-only; log it live.

### 1.8 Regime switching logic: deciding which family runs

The evidence above converges on one rule: **regime picks the strategy; the strategy picks the entry.** Trend continuation in Bull/Bear with ADX > 20; mean reversion toward the regime in Mixed or low-ADX; breakout only as a continuation trigger after compression; sweeps/CHoCH only as confirmation for a reversal that a *different* family proposed. The Hurst-based switch (trend when H > 0.55, buy-and-hold/fade when lower) has equity evidence only ([APJM](https://apjm.apacific.org/PDFs/12-109.pdf)); the 200MA/ADX switch has the broadest support and is already computed.

**Rules to implement (section 1)**

- R1.1 `--mode-select regime|blend` (new). In `regime` mode build the composite per strategy family: if `regimeInfo.label` is Bull/Bear and blended ADX >= 20, zero the weights of `momentum`, `bollinger` and `divergences` for counter-regime readings (an RSI < 30 in a Bear regime is continuation, as the trending-bear branch already encodes when `macroBias` exists) and double the `marketStructure`/`htf`/`regime` block. If label is Mixed or ADX < 20, halve `marketStructure`/`htf` and restore `momentum`/`bollinger` to full weight. Expected effect: fewer, more aligned signals; test net R and WAIT share.
- R1.2 `--mr-with-regime 1` (new). Mean-reversion votes (RSI, Stoch, lower/upper band, bullish/bearish divergence) count only when they point *toward* the regime direction or the regime is Mixed. Pure mirror of the existing "trending bull + RSI > 70 is momentum" branch, with `regimeInfo.label` standing in for the null `macroBias` in the candles path.
- R1.3 `--squeeze-release 1` (new). When `squeeze` was "volatility_compression" within the last N=6 15m bars and the current bar closes beyond the 20-bar Donchian band in the regime direction with ADX rising, add a continuation vote of +25 (sign by direction) in `marketStructure` and switch the entry to market. Test on 1h-aggregated decisions as well, since the evidence says 60m breakout bars are worst and 720m best.
- R1.4 `--structural-weight 0|1` (new). Zero the `liquiditySweep` component inside `scoreShortSideComposite` as a vote while keeping it in the `STRUCTURAL` confirmation set. If net R does not fall, the vote was noise.
- R1.5 `--session-bucket 1` (new, reporting only). Add `byHourUTC` and `byWeekday` summaries to the report. No scoring change until the buckets show a concentration.
- R1.6 `--avwap-location 1` (new). Compute AVWAP from the last confirmed 4h swing low (for longs) / high (for shorts) using `vwapBands`; add distance-in-ATR to the `marketStructure` location term (±15 within 1 ATR of AVWAP in the regime direction) and use AVWAP as a candidate level in the support/resistance list.

## 2. Multi-timeframe alignment and combining indicators without double counting

### 2.1 What MTF alignment adds, and what double counts

The one clean test (Quantpedia, section 1.1) shows the daily filter working as a **gate** that removes counter-trend trades, with risk-adjusted return doubling while trade count halved. It did not add the daily trend as another additive vote. The engine does both: `scoreHTF` adds three trend votes plus an alignment bonus *and* the HTF bear gate blocks shorts *and* `computeRegime` adds a 4h/1d vote *and* the counter-regime gate blocks. A LONG in a bull regime therefore collects the same information three or four times in the sum and in the gates. The alignment that adds value is the gate; the votes are redundant with `marketStructure` on the decision timeframe once the gate has fired.

Vendor claims that MTF alignment lifts win rates to 65-75% have no methodology ([VT Markets](https://www.vtmarkets.com/en-ca/discover/multi-timeframe-analysis-the-complete-trading-guide/)); ignore them.

### 2.2 Combining indicators: orthogonal blocks, ranks, votes

Grinold's law: IR ≈ IC × √breadth, with breadth counting *independent* bets; five trend proxies are one bet ([quantmemo](https://quantmemo.com/writing/paper-grinold-fundamental-law), [Panagora](https://www.panagora.com/assets/JOIM-Active-Risk-and-Information-Ratio.pdf)). The practical literature addresses redundancy with feature selection before combination, and weighted-majority voting with weights set from recent performance beats both single indicators and plain majority vote ([Numin, arXiv 2412.03167](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.03167), [Ensemble classifier 45 indicators](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08839514.2021.2001178)). Nobody has published a head-to-head of votes vs weighted sums on correlated indicators; the right answer for a small-sample engine is the one with fewer free parameters.

Recommended structure: six orthogonal blocks, each a single -100..100 score built from its own indicators, then a *rank-based* or vote combination across blocks rather than 18 weights:

| Block | Owns | Currently spread across |
|---|---|---|
| Regime/trend | 200MA, cloud, ADX/DI, EMA stack, Supertrend, HTF EMA trend | marketStructure, htf, regime, taSystem trend |
| Momentum/exhaustion | RSI, Stoch, MACD hist, divergences, TD Sequential, Elliott wave position | momentum, divergences, elliott, taSystem exhaustion |
| Location | distance to swing S/R, AVWAP, BB position, Donchian position, prior day H/L | marketStructure (near support), bollinger |
| Volatility state | BB width percentile, ATR percentile, squeeze, Keltner-inside-BB | squeeze gate, regime damping |
| Flow | volume ratio/trend, CVD proxy, volume climax, absorption | volume |
| Macro | regime lean, event, confirmation, catalyst | macro, event, confirmation, catalyst |

Within a block, use the *median* of the member signs scaled by the strongest magnitude (robust to one indicator going wild). Across blocks, two options to test: (a) equal-weight sum of block scores, (b) vote count = number of blocks with |score| >= 25 agreeing with the sign of the regime/trend block, with confidence from the vote count. Option (b) has one parameter.

### 2.3 Confidence from votes, not from the sum

With blocks in place, "A+ needs 3 strong categories" becomes "A+ needs regime + two more blocks agreeing". That matches what `strongCategories` tries to do today but removes the double count of redundant categories inflating the count.

**Rules to implement (section 2)**

- R2.1 `--htf-gate-both 1` (new). Mirror `shortNeedsHtfBear` as `longNeedsHtfBull` (LONG requires 4h bull, or 4h mixed with 1h bull). The asymmetric gate today is a long bias by construction; the test tells you how much of the long-biased edge is real.
- R2.2 `--htf-vote 0` (new). With the HTF gate on, zero `weights.htf` (the vote is now redundant with the gate). Expect near-identical net R with lower confidence inflation.
- R2.3 `--blocks 1` (new). Implement the six-block composite in `scoreFromCandles` behind a flag; report side-by-side with the 18-weight composite on the same 60/120-day windows.
- R2.4 `--combine sum|vote` (new, requires R2.3). `vote`: confidence = 50 + 10 × (agreeing blocks) with a minimum of 3 agreeing blocks to publish; `sum`: equal-weight block mean.
- R2.5 `--block-agg median|mean` (new). Intra-block aggregation; median is expected to generalize better.

## 3. Macro integration

### 3.1 Event-window behaviour: what the evidence says per asset

**BTC and CPI.** Sensitivity is regime-dependent and concentrated at one hour. Block Scholes: full sample since 2020, a 1pp upside CPI surprise maps to about -5.5% BTC over the next hour (R² ~25%); peak Dec 2021-Nov 2023 β about -13%/pp, R² 52%; mid-2026 β -3.2%/pp, R² 22%, with the rolling 12-release R² near 80% as inflation moved back above 4%; the 2026 relationship is "specific to the immediate one-hour horizon and not the 4-24h periods" ([Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again)). Older daily-data academic work found no significant CPI or GDP effect but did find unemployment and durable goods effects ([Corbet et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S154461231930159X)), and 8:30 releases show no announcement premium on average ([arXiv 2301.10117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10117)).

**BTC and NFP.** R² peaked near 60% in mid-2024 (labour-market focus, pivot to cuts) and is near zero in 2026 ([Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again)). Which print matters follows what the Fed is focused on; the engine's `inflationFocus` regime is the right selector and should also *scale* NFP vs CPI relevance, not just amplify.

**BTC and FOMC.** Pre-announcement drift exists in daily data: BTC +0.96% the day before, about -1% on the day, nothing significant after ([Pyo & Lee 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S154461231930159X)). Tightening surprises identified from fed funds futures cost about 0.25% on meeting days, larger in bull markets ([Ma et al. 2022 via search](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344638917_Do_FOMC_and_macroeconomic_announcements_affect_Bitcoin_prices)). The NY Fed finds BTC unresponsive to short-rate surprises in a one-hour window ([NY Fed SR1052](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1052.pdf?sc_lang=en)). Practitioner tallies say BTC fell after 7 of 8 FOMC meetings in 2025 ("sell the news"), unverified ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/fomc-meetings-impact-on-crypto)). The equity pre-FOMC drift (49bp in the 24h before, 80% of annual returns 1994-2011) has faded since and survives mainly for press-conference meetings ([Lucca & Moench](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr512.html), [Disappearing drift](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612320315956)). Under the current Fed (fewer press conferences, no forward guidance in statements), inter-meeting speeches and minutes are carrying the news: the Oct 7, 2026 minutes moved only BTC among major assets ([Yahoo/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/fed-minutes-point-another-rate-180521715.html), [Babypips](https://babypips.com/news/headline-sept-fomc-minutes-support-hawkish-stance-2026-10-08)); the SF Fed finds press conferences had become the dominant policy-news source before this shift ([SF Fed wp2025-30](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp2025-30.pdf)).

**Where the surprise persists: in rates, not in BTC.** Post-FOMC drift in Treasuries builds for weeks (a 10bp funds surprise moves 10y yields ~1.7bp same day and ~14bp by day 50) ([Brooks, Katz, Lustig, NBER w25127](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25127)). Pre-announcement price drift before US macro news exists in bonds/FX ([ECB wp1901](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecbwp1901.en.pdf)). For BTC, hourly shocks show *overreaction* after negative shocks with reversal growing over 6-24h ("bad news is good news") ([Intraday Bitcoin price shocks](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15140326.2022.2151253)). So for BTC the directional information in a print is spent inside the hour and the next 6-24h lean, if anything, toward partial reversal of large down moves.

**Gold.** NFP: across ~35 releases gold moved about +$7-8 fifteen minutes after weak prints and about -$5 after strong ones, correlation with the surprise -0.57 at 15m, -0.53 at 1h, fading by 4h ([FXStreet Feb 2025](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-january-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202502061000), [Jul 2025](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-july-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202507311000)). Structural drivers: the real-yield link was strong 2010-2019 and statistically insignificant 2020-2024 as central-bank buying and safe-haven demand took over ([UM dissertation 2025](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/140667/1/2518EMABFI533005056469_1.pdf)); PIMCO still regresses log real gold on 10y real yield 2004-2025 ([PIMCO](https://www.pimco.com/us/en/resources/education/understanding-gold-prices)); DXY carries stable *incremental* predictive power and does not substitute for real yields ([MDPI 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7099/14/3/79)). Implication: for gold the confirmation layer (2Y real yield direction, DXY) matters more than the headline surprise, and the regime switch "safe-haven bid on" should override the rate channel when VIX/risk-off is active.

**WTI.** EIA crude surprise: 101 releases 2022-2023, release-minute correlation -0.645, direction right ~68% at the shortest hold, coin flip within 10 minutes, no usable information by 30 minutes; the 30-minute absolute move (66bp) is bigger than the release-minute move (32bp) but less of it is explained by the surprise ([Marrazzo](https://antoniomarrazzo.substack.com/p/the-eia-crude-surprise-is-worth-20)). Academic intraday work: EIA shocks larger and longer than API, largest when API and consensus erred on the same side ([Energy Economics 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140988316302110)); all inventory surprises move returns inversely 2012-2018 ([IJFE 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/ijfiec/v29y2024i2p1513-1533.html)). OPEC: only *cuts* carry significant abnormal returns, with drift that continues the day after the meeting; increases have no clear effect when prices are high; CAARs positive over (-5,+5) disappear over (-20,+20) ([Demirer & Kutan](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S030142150900812X), [Loutia et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421515302007), [EUR thesis](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/37041/Elias-A.-353318-.pdf)). Geopolitics: June 2025 Israel-Iran took WTI $67 to $76 in weeks and back to pre-conflict within weeks of the ceasefire ([Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2025/0821)); a 15-event study 1990-2025 titled "the vanishing conflict premium" sorts by realized supply impact ([Cogent 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23322039.2026.2732750)); premiums persist only with real supply loss or a conflict past the 4-6 week mark ([Allianz](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_global/news-insights/economic-insights/Conflict_Middle_East_implications_markets_macro.html), [Fed note Oct 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/convenience-yields-risk-premiums-and-the-interpretation-of-oil-futures-prices-during-geo-conflict-20261009.html)).

### 3.2 How to use this: surprise as gate vs feature, sizing, windows

The engine's event score carries a directional vote for 6h with a 2h half-life. The evidence says the direction is worth something for about 60 minutes for BTC CPI, 10 minutes for WTI EIA, 1-4h for gold NFP, and then the market's *confirmation* (2Y, DXY, SPX) is the only thing still informative. The pre-event and the first minutes are volatility, not direction.

Recommended split:

1. **Gate (always):** no new entries from T-60min to T+10min for high/critical prints (currently ±30 min via EVENT_BLACKOUT_MS, and the 15-minute 0.85 cap). Widen the pre window, shorten the post window.
2. **Directional feature (short window):** event score at full weight T+10 to T+60min for BTC/GOLD, T+2 to T+15min for WTI inventories, *only if* confirmation status is "confirming". Then decay with a 45-minute half-life, not 2h, and drop to zero at 3h.
3. **Regime feature (long window):** after the window the print updates `classifyRegime` inputs (2Y change, DXY change, inflation focus), and the regime does the rest. The daily-regime bonus already exists.
4. **Sizing:** size multiplier 0.5 for entries inside T+10 to T+60, and stops widened 1.3x (the 30-minute range after CPI/NFP is about twice the pre-release range per a 24-release 2025-2026 sample ([TradeOS](https://www.tradeos.xyz/gold-xauusd-news-trading-strategy-cpi-nfp-2026))).
5. **Reversal bias after large BTC down shocks:** an hourly shock beyond 2.5 ATR down with regime not Bear gets a mean-reversion-long flag for the next 6-24h; this is the one documented post-shock drift for BTC.

### 3.3 Surprise z-score construction and prediction-market priors

Surprise vs consensus is the standard, but Kalshi's CPI markets had ~40% lower error than consensus Feb 2023 to mid-2025 and ~50% lower in shocks above 0.1pp; the Fed's own review found a statistically significant Kalshi edge for headline CPI and parity for core CPI and unemployment ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/12/22/prediction-markets-beat-wall-street-in-forecasting-inflation-kalshi-says), [Kalshi crisis alpha](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/crisis-alpha), [Fed via Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/federal-reserve-says-prediction-markets-are-a-valuable-tool-for-policymakers-2000724062)); independent reviews call prediction-market accuracy more mixed ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/markets/polymarket-kalshi-prediction-markets-not-so-reliable-says-study/)). Use: when a market-implied distribution exists for a print, compute z against the market-implied mean (blend 0.6 market / 0.4 consensus) and use the market's implied SD instead of `typicalSurpriseSD`. The surprise that moves price is the one relative to what *positioned* money expected.

### 3.4 Macro regime as strategy selector

`classifyRegime` yields inflationFocus, policyBias, risk. Map to families: risk-off + tightening favours short continuation and disfavours mean-reversion longs in BTC; risk-on + easing favours trend longs and buy-the-dip; inflation-focus high scales CPI relevance up and NFP down (and the reverse when the labour market is the focus, as in 2024). This is the only place where the macro score should change *which* price-action family is active; it should not keep adding ±20 to a sum.

### 3.5 Cross-asset confirmation after a print

For BTC the confirming instruments are, in order, 2Y yield direction, DXY, then SPX/NQ futures; for gold the 10y real yield proxy (TIPS ETF or 10y minus breakeven) and DXY; for WTI the curve (front spread) and DXY. The engine's `evaluateConfirmation` already produces confirming/fading/reversing. Make "reversing" a hard veto of the event vote and "fading" a 0.3 multiplier within the short window, which is what the code does; the change is to *not* carry the vote past the window at all.

**Rules to implement (section 3)**

Macro feeds are null in the candles-only harness, so these are tested with a *synthetic event calendar* (known release timestamps, no actuals): the harness needs `--events data/events.json` to load timestamps and importance, and `--event-window pre,post` to apply the gate. Direction effects need the live log.

- R3.1 `--event-window 60,10` (new). No new entries from T-60 to T+10 for importance high/critical. Compare with the current 30/30 on the same windows; measure net R of trades opened inside each band to see whether entries near prints lose.

## 4. Stops, targets and holding period

### 4.1 Evidence

The largest exit study available (Davey: 40 futures, 5 bar sizes 60-1440 min, 5 entries, 15 exits × 9 settings, 567,000 tests, 2010-2020 with costs) ranks: stop-and-reverse best; fixed dollar profit target second; breakeven stop a close third with *smaller* breakeven thresholds better; trailing and parabolic stops a clear step down; Chandelier and yo-yo stops worst of the complex exits; time-based exits worse than stop-and-reverse in every sector, bar size and entry; dollar-denominated exits generally beat ATR-denominated ones; the combined stop+target variant was always the weakest of its three ([KJ Trading](https://kjtradingsystems.com/algo-trading-exits.html)). Quantified Strategies found a 5-10 day time exit improved a daily equity mean-reversion system, with diminishing returns recently ([Quantified Strategies exits](http://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/trading-exit-strategies/)). ATR trailing stops whipsaw outside strong trends and need a trend filter; multipliers 2-3 are preference not finding ([IncredibleCharts](https://www.incrediblecharts.com/indicators/atr_average_true_range_trailing_stops.php), [PyQuantLab](https://pyquantlab.medium.com/dynamic-trailing-stops-using-atr-2d3c4e95ddc0)). On R:R, break-even win rate is 1/(1+RR) (33% at 2:1, 25% at 3:1, a few points higher after costs); no study finds a universal optimum ([backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/risk-reward-ratio-trading-expectancy-relationship)).

### 4.2 Reading the engine's own numbers against that

The 60-day best config: net +0.20R, PF 1.29. Wider stops raised TP1 rate but lowered net R in every configuration, which is the signature of a system whose winners are capped by the TP ladder (fixed targets) while losers scale with the stop. The two cheapest improvements by evidence are (a) a breakeven move after TP1 (Davey's third-best exit, and the engine already exits at TP1 on a stop after TP1, so breakeven is a small variation that removes the "stop_after_tp" give-back), and (b) a horizon tied to the setup timeframe instead of a flat 24h. Trailing stops should be tested but expected to underperform the ladder. Time stops should be *loosened*, not tightened: the 24h horizon on a 4h/1d continuation setup is the likely cause of the TP3 rate being low.

Structure-based stops (behind the swing) are right in principle but swing pivots with lookback 2 on 15m are noise; the stop should hide behind the *4h* swing or AVWAP when the setup is a continuation, and behind the 15m/1h swing only for mean-reversion entries. Expected hold: 15m setups 4-12h, 1h 12-36h, 4h 2-6 days, 1d 1-3 weeks; the horizon should be about 3x the setup timeframe's ATR-to-TP2 travel estimate.

**Rules to implement (section 4)**

- R4.1 `--breakeven-after-tp1 1` (new). After TP1 trades, move the stop to entry + cost; a stop after that exits at breakeven rather than at TP1. Compare the stop_after_tp bucket.
- R4.2 `--horizon-hours 24|48|96` and `--horizon-by-tf 1` (new). `--horizon-by-tf` sets 12h for setups whose dominant structure timeframe is 15m, 36h for 1h, 96h for 4h/1d (dominant timeframe = the TF whose taSystem component carries the largest |score|).
- R4.3 `--stop-anchor 15m|1h|4h|auto` (new). `auto`: continuation setups anchor the stop behind the nearest 4h swing or AVWAP (whichever is closer but >= minStop), mean-reversion setups behind the 1h swing.
- R4.4 `--tp-mode ladder|ladder-trail|atr` (new). `ladder-trail`: after TP2, trail at 2.5 × ATR(14, 1h) (Chandelier) instead of the fixed TP3. `atr`: TP1/2/3 at 1.5/2.5/4 ATR regardless of levels. Expect `ladder` to win per the evidence; the test is cheap and settles it.
- R4.5 `--min-rr 1.0|1.5` (new). Skip signals whose TP2 reward-to-risk is below the threshold. Current `riskReward` is reported but not gated.
- R4.6 Report `holdHoursMedian` by outcome and by dominant TF in `summarize` so the horizon rule can be set from data.

## 5. Validation methodology for a small-sample engine

### 5.1 Multiple testing and the 36-config sweep

A 36-trial sweep on 60 days produces a best-of-36 Sharpe that is inflated by selection. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado) corrects it: compute the expected maximum Sharpe of N trials, SR₀ ≈ √V · [(1−γ)·Z⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Z⁻¹(1−1/(N·e))] with γ = 0.5772 and V the variance of the trials' Sharpes, then test the chosen SR against SR₀ with the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio, PSR(SR₀) = Z[ (SR − SR₀)·√(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR + (γ₄−1)/4·SR²) ], where T is the number of observations, γ₃ skew and γ₄ kurtosis of the per-trade returns ([Bailey & López de Prado, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551), [davidhbailey.com](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). Minimum track record length: MinTRL = 1 + [1 − γ₃·SR + (γ₄−1)/4·SR²]·(Z_α / (SR − SR*))², the number of trades needed for SR to be distinguishable from benchmark SR* at confidence α.

Worked numbers for the engine: ~200 closed trades, mean +0.20R, per-trade SD plausibly ~1.1R gives a per-trade SR of 0.18 and a raw t ≈ 0.18·√200 ≈ 2.6. With N = 36 trials whose Sharpes vary with SD ~0.08, SR₀ ≈ 0.08·2.1 ≈ 0.17, so the chosen config is barely above the luck benchmark; PSR against SR₀ is ~0.55, not 0.95. At the same SR, the MinTRL to beat zero at 95% is about (1.645/0.18)² ≈ 85 trades; to beat SR₀ = 0.17 it is in the thousands. The honest read: the sweep shows the config is *not worse* than the rest, not that it has edge, which matches the engine's own comment.

### 5.2 Walk-forward, purging, embargo, CPCV

The harness is already walk-forward at the bar level (no lookahead in candles). What it lacks is *parameter* walk-forward: choose on window k, evaluate on window k+1, roll. With 15m decisions and 24h+ horizons, labels overlap; any k-fold must purge training samples whose outcome window overlaps a test sample and embargo a horizon-length gap after each test block ([purged CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation), [purged-cross-validation library](https://github.com/eslazarev/purged-cross-validation)). Combinatorial purged CV generates many backtest paths and gives a distribution of out-of-sample performance rather than one number; a 2024 comparison found it ahead of k-fold and walk-forward on both probability of backtest overfitting and deflated Sharpe ([methodology](https://eslazarev.github.io/purged-cross-validation/methodology/)). For this engine: 6 blocks of 30 days over 180 days, 2 test blocks per split (15 paths), 24h embargo.

### 5.3 Regime-conditional evaluation

Every result must be reported by regime label (Bull/Bear/Mixed/Transition), by alignment, by calendar month, by session hour, and by setup type (continuation / reversal_confirmed / reversal_forming). A config that wins only in Bull with aligned longs is a long-only trend system and should be labelled as such. The Jun-Aug vs Aug-Oct split already proves regime dependence; the next step is to make it a standard output so a change that "improves net R" can be checked for whether it improved the weak regime or just the strong one.

### 5.4 Minimum samples

Practitioner thresholds range from 30 to 1,000 trades ([trading.dude](https://medium.com/@trading.dude/how-many-trades-are-enough-a-guide-to-statistical-significance-in-backtesting-093c2eac6f05), [targethit](https://targethit.com/learn/sample-size)); the correct answer is the MinTRL above, which for a +0.2R / 1.1R-SD system is ~85 trades for "beats zero" and far more for "beats the best of 36". Per-bucket conclusions (per symbol, per regime) need that count *in the bucket*. Any bucket under 40 trades is descriptive only.

### 5.5 Calibration of confidence (Brier)

Confidence is 50 + |score|/2, a monotone transform with no probabilistic meaning. Calibration: bin published signals by confidence (55-59, 60-64, 65-69, 70-74, 75+), compute the TP1-before-stop rate per bin and the Brier score against the hit outcome; compare with the constant base-rate predictor. With a few hundred samples use Platt scaling (a logistic map from confidence to P(TP1)); isotonic overfits below ~2,000 samples and introduces rank ties ([Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.1403), [metricgate](https://metricgate.com/docs/calibration-isotonic-platt/), [letsdatascience](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/probability-calibration-why-high-accuracy-doesn-t-mean-you-can-trust-your-model)). The calibrated P(TP1) should then drive grade and size instead of raw confidence; if the Platt slope is near zero the confidence number carries no information and the publish threshold is arbitrary.

**Rules to implement (section 5)**

- R5.1 `--report-buckets regime,month,hour,setup` (new). Extend `summarize` groupings; include trade count and the per-bucket t-stat of net R.
- R5.2 `--sweep-deflate 1` (new, or a post-processing script over `data/backtests/*.json`). Reads all configs of a sweep, computes per-trade SR, skew, kurtosis, SR₀ for N configs, PSR and MinTRL, and prints them next to net R. Any config whose PSR(SR₀) < 0.90 is reported as "not distinguishable from selection luck".
- R5.3 `--wfo 30,6` (new). Rolling parameter walk-forward: for each of 6 consecutive 30-day blocks, pick the best of the sweep on block k and record its out-of-sample net R on block k+1; report the OOS sum. This is the number to trust.
- R5.4 `--cpcv 6,2,24` (new). Combinatorial purged CV with 6 blocks, 2 test blocks, 24h embargo; report the distribution (median, 10th percentile) of OOS net R across the 15 paths.
- R5.5 `--calib 1` (new). Output the confidence-bin table, the Brier score, the base-rate Brier, and a fitted Platt slope/intercept from the backtest trades; the same script runs on the live log.
- R5.6 Decision rule for adopting any change: it must improve OOS net R in R5.3 *and* not reduce the weakest regime bucket's net R by more than 0.05R *and* keep closed-trade count within 30% of baseline.
